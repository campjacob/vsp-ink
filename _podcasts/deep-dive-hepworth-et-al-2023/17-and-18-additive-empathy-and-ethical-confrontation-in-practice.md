  Moving into the change-oriented phase of the helping process, this episode looks at how social workers help clients see what they are not saying and work through what stands in the way of change. The hosts explain how additive empathy goes beyond reciprocal empathy to reach for underlying feelings, and how interpretation identifies patterns, goals, and wishes that clients imply but do not state, offered tentatively, sparingly, and only once a working relationship is in place. They then turn to confrontation as an ethical intervention, from fostering self-confrontation through questioning to the four elements of assertive confrontation, and to when safety requires confronting a client right away. The second half covers barriers to change: social worker under- and overinvolvement, transference and countertransference, cross-racial and cross-cultural barriers and broaching, reactance and motivational interviewing, and therapeutic binds. Listeners will come away with a clearer sense of how to challenge clients while keeping self-awareness at the center of their practice.
