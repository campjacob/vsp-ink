  A diagnosis names a problem, but a social work assessment tries to understand the whole person, and this episode works through how that happens. The hosts describe assessment as both a written product and an ongoing working hypothesis that changes as trust builds, and they look at what to do when a client's own account conflicts with what collateral contacts report. They distinguish the presenting problem from the problem for work, explain why the meaning a client gives a problem shapes the intervention, and walk through the ROPES framework for drawing out strengths. From there they cover anti-oppressive practice and goodness of fit, then the biophysical, cognitive and perceptual, affective, and behavioral areas of individual functioning, including the CAGE questions and suicide risk. The episode closes on the environment, from daily living tasks to formal and informal supports and faith communities, and a reminder that a written assessment can be subpoenaed.
