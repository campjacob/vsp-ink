  This episode was generated using Gemini Notebook from Chapter 6, "Ethical Responsibilities to the Social Work Profession," and Chapter 7, "Ethical Responsibilities to the Broader Society," in Reamer's Ethical Standards in Social Work: A Review of the NASW Code of Ethics. It is designed to help you engage with and review the material, not to replace the reading.


  A social worker's ethical obligations do not end at the door of the counseling room, and this episode follows them outward, first to the profession and then to society. It begins with Standard 5.01's call for responsible criticism of the profession, using the Tarasoff decision and the early HIV/AIDS crisis as moments when prevailing norms on confidentiality had to change. The hosts then turn to evaluation and research under Standard 5.02, including informed consent, the right to withdraw, and why a researcher's deadlines never outweigh a participant's well-being. From there they take up the standards on social welfare, public participation, public emergencies, and social and political action, including the limits on how much a social worker is expected to give in an emergency and Gil's definition of oppression. Listeners will come away seeing how even a clinician in private practice can devote part of their professional time to the broader cause.
