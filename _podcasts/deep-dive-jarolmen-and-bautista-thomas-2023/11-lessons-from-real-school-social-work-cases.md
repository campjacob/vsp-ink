  Chapter 11 is built from real practice vignettes, and this episode works through them as a bridge from classroom theory to the realities of a school placement. The hosts start with the idea that intervening with one student is also work on the school climate, then turn to two students who made violent threats, Joe and James. Along the way they walk through the four areas of a threat assessment and explain why a strengths perspective and cognitive therapy still matter alongside safety measures. The cases of Ana and Nicky show interventions that did not succeed because a student could not accept their circumstances, while Mary's memory box and Carol's fear of abandonment show the same tools working. Later segments cover how the COVID-19 pandemic shifted school social work toward meeting basic needs, and the emotional toll the work takes, including burnout and the self-care the chapter recommends. Listeners will come away with a clearer picture of both the weight and the lasting impact of school social work.
