  This episode was generated using Gemini Notebook from Chapter 7, "Being a Culturally Responsive School Social Worker," in Jarolmen and Bautista-Thomas's School Social Work: A Direct Practice Guide. It is designed to help you engage with and review the material, not to replace the reading.


  Cultural responsiveness is presented here as a lifelong posture rather than a skill to check off, and this episode traces what that means for school social workers. It starts with the 2017 NASW change from cultural competence to cultural awareness, then turns inward to self-assessment through the Multicultural Awareness, Knowledge, and Skills Survey and to reflexivity about power. The hosts look at how language and culture can be mistaken for cognitive deficits, using the chapter's example of a recent immigrant from Portugal whose evaluation had to happen in her native language, and why an independent interpreter, never a child or sibling, is required. They discuss the pressures on immigrant and refugee students, including high-stakes testing and grade retention, and the nepantlera-informed paradigm drawn from Gloria Anzaldua's work. The episode closes with Dupper's steps for changing racist behavior in schools, how poverty drives absenteeism, and how to respect students' religious practice without probing private beliefs.
