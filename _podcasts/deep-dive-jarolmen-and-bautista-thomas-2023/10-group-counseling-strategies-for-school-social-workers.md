  This episode was generated using Gemini Notebook from Chapter 10, "Engagement, Assessment, and Intervention Skills in the School for Groups Using a Variety of Practice Methods," in Jarolmen and Bautista-Thomas's School Social Work: A Direct Practice Guide. It is designed to help you engage with and review the material, not to replace the reading.


  This episode looks at why group work in schools is more than a way to see more students at once. It begins with the chapter's case for groups, including the estimate that 75% of children with emotional difficulties do not receive services, then works through Gazda's definition of group counseling and what permissiveness can mean inside a structured school. The hosts discuss group size by age, homogeneous and heterogeneous selection, the pre-group interview, rotating meeting times so students do not miss the same class each week, a private room, and time at the end of a session before students return to class. They compare situational and topic-oriented groups and the group types the chapter describes, and explain why psychotherapy groups rarely fit a school. The episode closes with brief cognitive-behavioral and solution-focused groups, Banks's approach, and social mapping as a way to work across cultures. Confidentiality, the stages of groups, single-session groups, and group evaluation, also in the reading, are not covered here.
