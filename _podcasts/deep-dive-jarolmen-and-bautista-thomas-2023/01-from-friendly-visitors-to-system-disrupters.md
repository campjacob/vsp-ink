  This episode was generated using Gemini Notebook from Chapter 1, "An Overview of the Theoretical Information Necessary in the Field of School Social Work," in Jarolmen and Bautista-Thomas's School Social Work: A Direct Practice Guide. It is designed to help you engage with and review the material, not to replace the reading.


  The school social worker's job in 1913 was to check tenements and make sure children were in their seats; today it is to disrupt the systems that keep them out. The episode traces that change decade by decade — the Depression shifting the work toward food and shelter, the postwar years turning it inward toward students' psychology, the 1960s pushing it back outward toward racism and neglect, and COVID-19 returning it to food insufficiency and housing — alongside the legal record that forced it: Brown v. Board, Section 504, PL 94-142 and its evolution into IDEA, and No Child Left Behind, which tied federal funding to test scores and made removing socioeconomic barriers a matter of institutional survival. The second half turns to the chapter's theory, working through the systems approach, the ecological perspective, and the strengths perspective, pausing on whether a strengths focus risks minimizing systemic trauma, and closing on positionality.
