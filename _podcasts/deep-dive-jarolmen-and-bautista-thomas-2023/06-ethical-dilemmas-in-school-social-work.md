  School social workers practice in a host environment, where the building's mission is education and theirs is the safety and well-being of students, and this episode looks at the ethical decisions that tension produces. It opens with three lenses for decision-making: deontological, teleological, and social constructivist, illustrated by a case where a grandmother's consent turns out not to be valid. It then takes up confidentiality and the "need to know" in schools, the difference between telling and tattling, the duty to warn established in Tarasoff, and FERPA, along with the importance of explaining the limits of confidentiality at the first session. A scenario about a weekend party is worked through Raines's seven-step process, and the ethical principles screen is presented as a way to rank competing values, with protection of life at the top. The episode closes on dual relationships, the obligation to challenge systemic inequities, and keeping sessions confidential in e-therapy.
