  This episode was generated using Gemini Notebook from Chapter 9, Section II, "Engagement, Assessment, and Intervention Skills in the School for Individuals Using Evidence-Based Practice Modalities and Other Practice Methods," in Jarolmen and Bautista-Thomas's School Social Work: A Direct Practice Guide. It is designed to help you engage with and review the material, not to replace the reading.


  This episode asks what a school social worker does when standard practice is not enough, moving from acute crisis to systemic supports. It traces crisis intervention to Erich Lindemann's work after the Cocoanut Grove fire and uses Roberts's definition of crisis as a disequilibrium that a student's usual coping cannot resolve. The hosts then turn to attachment, explaining why "school phobia" is usually separation anxiety and how a teacher or the social worker can become a secure base, through daily check-ins or a plant sent home over the summer. They describe play therapy as a way for children to show what they cannot yet say, and school-linked services as a way to bring health and mental health care into the building. The textbook's case of Joe ties these together, showing how a counseling referral his family could not reach shaped what followed. Restorative justice, intrapsychic humanism, and hip hop therapy, also in the reading, are not covered here.
