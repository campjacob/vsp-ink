  When a student acts out, fails classes, or withdraws, the cause is often far bigger than the student. This episode works through the societal issues Chapter 8 surveys and asks how often schools read those forces as individual misbehavior. The hosts start with poverty, which the chapter frames as a failure of society rather than of the child, then turn to homelessness and the McKinney-Vento Act's promise of school stability. They discuss children of incarcerated parents, the child welfare system and racial disproportionality in who gets reported, and the contradictions facing undocumented students, including Igoa's four needs of immigrant children. They also cover children raised by LGBTQ parents, bullying, youth suicide, and why the chapter finds punitive, zero-tolerance responses to school violence less effective than supportive interventions and positive classroom environments. Listeners will come away better prepared to look past a behavior to the circumstances driving it, and to see the school social worker's role as advocating with the systems around a child.
